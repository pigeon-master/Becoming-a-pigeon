# Wizard visitor

The outlined stick figure below the human icon wears a tall top hat. Clicking it requests front-camera permission and opens a small mirrored preview. A taller, broader blond Western male magician enters in a white suit, navy shirt, red tie, white gloves/shoes, white cape, tall white top hat with a navy band, and exactly one silver monocle with a chain. The model reuses the original human's smooth photo-projected body. Its icon has a taller hat with a wider brim and no cape outline.

After the rope descent, landing and turn toward the camera, the mirrored palm position controls the whole arm in nine directions, including a held stationary hand. Center raises both arms forward and regroups the flock; left/right and downward positions steer walking pigeons. Upper positions lift the whole flock into sustained synchronized flight, including diagonal flight. Lowering or losing the hand lands the flock smoothly. The signal is smoothed to reduce jitter and small deviations around screen center have a dead zone. The flock stays within the central plaza and retains collision steering. Movement is local to the viewer; no positions, frames or landmarks are sent to Supabase.

Waving both hands continuously like a conductor independently poses both arms and triggers a rising flock vortex. Merely holding two hands, or moving only one, does not trigger it. Each arm retains a short activity history so alternating beats at low inference rates also count; losing either hand clears the two-hand conducting signal immediately. Duplicate palm detections and repeated handedness labels are reduced to one hand, and two hands must remain visible for at least 300 ms before conducting can accumulate. The vortex is a pair of turbulent rotating spirals spanning most of the viewport, with depth and height variations, banked birds and fast wingbeats. Its positions are derived from the active camera frustum to adapt to desktop and portrait screens. A 3D spacing pass pushes birds apart instead of resolving their aerial positions as ground collisions. Its short momentum tail fades after conducting stops, returning to directional flight or a gradual landing. The control/camera session lasts 60 seconds after the magician lands and turns toward the camera. Entry uses a 2.9-second overhead rope descent in a one-arm hanging pose. The rope retracts after landing. On exit it lowers again, the wizard grips it, and both rise overhead; the complete exit lasts 2.5 seconds. Rope geometry and material are disposed with the visitor. A wall-clock camera timer also caps the control session on devices with slow rendering. Its close button, tab hiding, permission failure, or inference failure stops the camera. Normal pigeon speeds and the original camera position are restored after exit.

## Performance and compatibility

Up to two hands are tracked. Camera capture requests a wide 640 × 360 frame at 24 fps. Worker input is at most 480 × 360, switching to 320 × 240 when inference cost exceeds 65 ms. CPU inference runs in a classic Web Worker at up to about 12 fps, with an adaptive cycle based on measured inference cost. Only one frame can be in flight. Tracking assets are loaded on icon activation, not on page startup. The new hand model is about 7.8 MB, the existing local WASM files are reused, and the photo atlas is about 0.68 MB. Closing terminates the worker and stops all camera tracks.

Use HTTPS (or localhost), grant camera permission, and use a browser supporting camera capture, ImageBitmap and MediaPipe in Web Workers. In-app browsers or older phones may fail or run more slowly. There is no guarantee of equal performance on every device; full body tracking would add more work and is unnecessary for this hand-swipe interaction. Keeping inference in a worker, limiting its frequency, and reducing image resolution avoids competing with the 3D scene on its UI thread. See [Google's Hand Landmarker web guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker/web_js).

> Create a photorealistic 3D character texture atlas for a website. Both figures standing in a strict horizontal T-pose with arms straight out to the sides, palms forward, full hands and shoes visible, equal size and aligned feet/head. Neutral soft diffuse studio lighting, orthographic camera, realistic cloth and skin. Costume: tailored white three-piece suit, white jacket and trousers, dark navy blue shirt, deep red necktie, white gloves, white dress shoes. No bag or glasses. DO NOT include a hat or a cape in the atlas: these are separately modeled. Transparent background around the bodies, no labels, no text, no shadow on a backdrop. Keep the front face centered and clearly defined for projection on a smooth 3D volume. Both figures entirely inside separate equal-width half-panels. Costume reference is the classic white-suited stage magician with navy blue shirt and red tie; render it as a real live-action adult man.

## Validation

`npm.cmd run test:wizard` checks directional flock movement with 40 pigeons, arm poses, hat/monocle presence, desktop/portrait exit and camera restoration. It also runs the actual MediaPipe worker against a fake camera, verifies resource release, and handles denied camera permission. It does not validate tracking accuracy on a physical phone. `npm.cmd run test:human` checks that the original human, dog and car interactions still work.

Latest built-in image_gen edit prompt (saved over public/models/wizard-photo-atlas.png):

> Edit this character texture atlas in place. Change ONLY the hair color on both the front and back photographs from dark brown to natural golden blond, including sideburns. Preserve exactly the same Western adult male identity, face, hairstyle, skin, white suit, blue shirt, red tie, white gloves and shoes, T-pose, scale, positions, full-body front/back panel composition and transparent background. Do not add a hat, cape or accessories because these are separate 3D meshes. Maintain photorealism and all existing alpha transparency.

The wizard has an upright neck, a smaller head with a longer square jaw, a muscular torso and wider strides. The white cape is pinned at both shoulders, follows movement with spring-damped fabric folds and stays above the ground. After arriving at the flock center, the wizard turns smoothly toward the camera. The instruction reads: Move your hand to guide the pigeons. It remains visible for 20 seconds after the magician has landed and finished turning, regardless of hand gestures. Palm size relative to its initial apparent size approximates approaching the camera and steers pigeons toward the viewer; regular control keeps them near the wizard.

Control keeps pigeon anchors inside a padded viewport after collision/spacing corrections, preserves normal-flight altitude when correcting edges, and limits per-frame displacement. Wizard ground contact corrections use three passes per frame instead of the normal flock's twenty; the default flock physics stays unchanged. The wizard face projection reduces feature size and includes the chin region below the lower lip. The monocle follows the adjusted eye position. Browser regression covers 40-bird diagonal flight, camera approach, landing and displacement limits in desktop and portrait views.

Directional control moves a loose flock toward the hand-directed group destination instead of continuously pushing every bird into the screen border. Individual phased destinations vary in depth and position and curve inward before the edge, preserving a dispersed flock. The hard viewport bound remains only a safety correction. Browser validation also checks overhead arrival, hand-to-rope attachment, retraction and rope-assisted exit.

The rope extends four world units below the hand and its upper anchor is higher. On landing, the wizard holds the rope briefly, then smoothly lowers the shoulder, elbow, wrist and bent leg during the 1.1-second settling turn. Exit lowers the rope in 1.05 seconds, pauses briefly for the grip and ascends over 1.3 seconds. Browser validation checks continuity of the hand position across landing and completion of exit by 2.75 seconds.

When no hand command is active, pigeons take independent short, slow steps and occasional pauses, choosing different headings and timings. This local idle movement remains inside the control area and yields immediately to synchronized hand control. Browser validation checks that several birds continue to move slowly while waiting for a hand signal.

Wizard and rope meshes do not cast directional shadows. A small soft contact shadow fades in beneath the feet only in the final 1.7 world units before landing and fades out during ascent. The instruction stays for the first 20 seconds of the control phase and is removed early when the visitor exits. Validation checks the shadow fade and persistence of the instruction after hand input.

Performance update: wizard control shares one physics-body list across the flock, reuses previous-position vectors, and clips destinations against homogeneous camera planes instead of repeated projection searches. Smooth skinning processes only influenced vertices and skips unchanged joint transforms. Wizard upper-body extraction uses resolution 48 instead of 64; cape geometry uses 24 × 28 subdivisions and updates at up to 30 Hz. During wizard visits the main world refreshes directional shadow maps at up to 20 Hz while continuing to animate birds each frame.

Input update: the palm center averages wrist and knuckle positions. Straight, extended fingers contribute a mirrored pointing vector; single-finger pointing takes priority, while an open palm mainly retains position control. Aspect ratio correction, time-based smoothing and a 220 ms loss grace reduce direction jumps. The scheduler skips duplicate video frames and subtracts elapsed inference/capture time from the wait, rather than adding a full delay after inference. Confidence thresholds are 0.4. Validation includes scripts/hand-tracking.test.ts and scripts/smooth-surface.test.ts in addition to the wizard and original visitor browser regressions. Actual camera recognition and frame rates still require testing on the target device.

Rapid sustained single-hand movement triggers a separate oscillating ribbon/fan formation, never the two-hand vortex. A single quick sweep is insufficient. Both formations shift with the current hand direction and camera-approach signal; faster gestures increase their spread. Single-hand input limits any preceding vortex tail to 250 ms. Controller tests cover duplicate detections, a lost second hand, brief detection noise, valid two-hand conducting and one-hand flourish. The 40-bird browser regression checks ribbon flight and translation on desktop and portrait views.

The quoted italic guide uses 17 px text on desktop and 13 px on mobile. It stays fully visible for 20 seconds, then smoothly fades to zero opacity over two seconds before being hidden. Browser validation samples opacity during both halves of the fade. The ownership delete control retains its original button ID, accessibility label and confirmation behavior, now rendered as a thin hand-drawn trash-can SVG.

Wizard trousers are now one blended surface from the waist into both legs, deforming with the hip and knee joints. The separate hip/front overlay and individual leg-cover meshes are removed from the wizard. White gloves use a thicker palm, connected finger shapes and a two-segment thumb with a visible web between thumb and fingers. Original human geometry remains unchanged. Browser validation checks that both the trousers and glove geometry each form exactly one connected component and verifies the increased palm depth.

The trouser waist now matches the shirt hem in width and front/back depth before gradually widening into the hips. The magician camera preview is 210 px wide on desktop and 156 px on mobile, uses a 16:9 frame and contains the entire camera image instead of cropping it. Capture prefers 16:9, with actual camera dimensions still used for tracking calculations. Left and right icon groups are inset by 16 px horizontally / 12 px vertically on desktop and 10 px on mobile, respecting device safe areas. Glove surfaces retain smooth field normals.

Waist refinement: the trouser hip section is rounded, upper-thigh centers taper inward, and the trouser waistband stays slightly inside the shirt to prevent coincident-surface artifacts. The wizard's lower torso is modestly widened. Arm skinning fades out below the upper chest so lowering or raising the arms cannot shrink or twist the shirt hem. A close waist screenshot is saved by the browser regression for visual review.

Hand-count refinement: tracking always supplies independent left/right arm poses, with zero strength for the absent arm. Confident handedness keeps the same arm active when a hand crosses the camera center and preserves two distinct hands held close together. The visit smooths each arm independently instead of overwriting both strengths with the global command. Shoulder compensation, elbow flexion and wrist tilt make pointing less rigid. Ordinary flock targets now move across a camera-sized range instead of staying within four units of the wizard, and the highest command raises the flock toward 10 units. Formation targets follow the moving group center with per-bird margins, while viewport safety remains active. Controller and desktop/portrait browser checks cover actual single/two-hand signals, joint articulation, wider lateral movement and higher flight. Real-world recognition still depends on lighting, occlusion and device camera quality.

Camera refinement: fit the normal 12-unit control envelope instead of the previous 18-unit envelope, so entry uses a closer view. Sideways targets use 60% of the viewport half-width, while forward/backward travel is capped at six units. Hand identity and independent arm control remain unchanged.

iPhone compatibility: explicitly provide an OffscreenCanvas to the worker task, bypassing MediaPipe's Safari user-agent guard that can incorrectly attempt document.createElement inside a worker. If worker initialization is unsupported, keep the camera and automatically initialize the same CPU hand model with a DOM canvas. This compatibility path downsizes frames to at most 320×240 and processes at most about 5.5 frames per second, further reducing frequency if inference is expensive. Supported browsers retain worker processing. Both paths close the task, stop capture tracks and clear the video source on exit. The hand-tracking browser regression runs real model inference with an iPhone in-app user agent and exercises the missing-OffscreenCanvas fallback in Chromium and WebKit; the desktop WebKit camera input is simulated because that runner has no media capture API.

Startup latency: request the camera and initialize hand tracking concurrently. Preload the magician photo when the icon is clicked, and begin the rope entrance as soon as video playback starts, without waiting for the hand model. The loading indicator remains until tracking is ready. An abort signal prevents a late photo download from creating a magician after cancellation, and initialization errors stop an already-running entrance. Browser checks delay the model response by 1.8 seconds and verify the camera-ready callback runs before tracking readiness on both worker and compatibility paths.
